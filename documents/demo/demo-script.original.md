# Demo Script (3 minutes)

About 325 spoken words, or roughly 1.8 words a second, which leaves room to pause on the
visuals. The first 7 seconds stack three surprises: two identical cats, a camera that confuses
them, and a ball mistaken for a cat.

**[0:00 · close-up of two white cats, then the ball with a "Qbo" box on it]**
These are Qb and Qbo. My camera can't tell them apart. Last week, it decided this *ball* was
Qbo, for twenty-six minutes.

**[0:08 · empty apartment, morning light]**
I work all day. I just want to know simple things: did my cats eat, sleep, play? Cats hide
illness, so small changes matter.

**[0:20 · cost counter ticking up]**
Why not send every frame to an AI? One camera makes over a hundred and seventy thousand frames
a day. That's about sixty-three thousand dollars a year. A tiny model on a Raspberry Pi is
almost free, but it makes mistakes.

**[0:40 · timeline chart; red escalation lines appear]**
So we built both. The Pi watches every frame. When it's unsure, it says exactly why. It knows
fourteen kinds of doubt, like "I relabeled this cat by distance alone."

**[1:00 · agent trace: the frames it picks, its reasoning]**
That doubt becomes a plain-English question for Meta's Muse Spark. The agent pulls the frames
it needs, compares the cats, and answers with a reason. Watch: "Thin upright tail, cool white
fur. This is Qb, not Qbo." Overturned.

**[1:25 · three cases in one trace; empty floor highlighted]**
Related doubts get reviewed together, fetching each image once. Twelve questions, three calls.
Here it caught the tracker inventing a cat on an empty floor, and deleted it.

**[1:45 · a corrected row, tagged with its verdict]**
Every verdict rewrites the record, and every change is traceable.

**[1:55 · the day log scrolling]**
Then memory. Not boxes and timestamps, but a diary: "Qb slept in the green bed, four
thirty-four to four forty." Over weeks, it learns what normal looks like for each cat, in each
spot.

**[2:15 · results card]**
The numbers: twelve doubts in eight minutes, less than one percent of frames. Twenty-six cents
to review them, about a fifth of the cost of sending every frame to the AI. And only those
frames ever leave my home.

**[2:35 · roadmap card]**
Next: catch more errors, run full days, and teach the Pi from what the agent learns.

**[2:45 · montage: elderly parent, wildlife, factory line]**
Cats are just the start. Any camera watching for rare moments, like an elderly parent,
wildlife or a factory line, has the same gap. Our answer: cheap eyes on every frame, a smart
brain only when it matters, and a memory that keeps growing. Built this week, on Meta's Muse
Spark.
